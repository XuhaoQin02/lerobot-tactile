Which Physical Tactile Cues Matter for Contact-Rich Imitation Learning?

A Controlled Real-Robot Study

Xuhao Qin, Xiyan Huang, Haoyu Dong, Xi Zhang, and Chenxi Xiao†

† Corresponding author, School of Information Science and Technology, ShanghaiTech University

PDF Overview Code

Abstract

Vision-based tactile sensors capture multiple aspects of physical interaction within the same image stream, including contact appearance, geometry, indentation depth, and tangential deformation. However, existing visuo-tactile policies often process tactile observations as ordinary images, making it difficult to determine which physical cues actually contribute to policy learning. We present a controlled real-robot study that isolates physically interpretable tactile signals using an open, LeRobot-compatible visuo-tactile platform. From the same tactile stream, we derive tactile RGB images, normal/depth maps, and marker-flow fields. Under identical demonstrations and policy backbones, we compare individual and combined tactile cues, tactile encoders, and visuo-tactile fusion strategies across three contact-rich manipulation tasks. Across 720 real-robot trials, full tactile improves aggregate success from 57.8% to 78.9%. Our results suggest that normal/depth cues support contact establishment, marker flow supports established-contact regulation, and combining cues improves continuity across interaction stages.

Overview

Physical tactile cues for contact-rich imitation learning

LeRobot-compatible platform

A unified hardware and data pipeline synchronizes global vision, wrist vision, robot state, actions, and tactile observations.

Controlled cue study

The same demonstrations and policy backbone isolate the contributions of appearance, geometry, and tangential deformation.

Real-robot evaluation

Three tasks cover in-gripper adjustment, sustained surface contact, and tight-clearance insertion.

System and Policy

A controlled Diffusion Policy pipeline keeps the demonstrations, visual observations, robot state, action space, and training budget fixed.

Tactile Signal Representations

01

Tactile RGB

Raw tactile appearance preserves local contact patterns and color changes induced by elastomer deformation.

02

Normal / Depth

Surface normals and reconstructed depth describe contact geometry and local indentation.

03

Marker Flow

Tracked marker displacement exposes tangential deformation and established-contact regulation.

Real-Robot Tasks

Tube reorientation and insertion

Adjust the tube inside the gripper and insert it into a holder despite partially occluded contact.

Blackboard wiping

Maintain stable surface contact while moving tangentially across the target region.

Peg-in-hole insertion

Align and insert under tight clearance, where small contact errors cause jamming or dropping.

Results

Controlled comparison of tactile representations

Full tactile raises aggregate success from 57.8% to 78.9% and achieves the strongest or joint-strongest result on every task.

Conclusion

Geometry helps establish contact, marker flow helps regulate established contact, and their combination supports smoother transitions across contact-rich interaction stages.
