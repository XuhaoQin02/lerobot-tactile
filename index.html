Which Physical Tactile Cues Matter for Contact-Rich Imitation Learning?

A Controlled Real-Robot Study

Xuhao Qin, Xiyan Huang, Haoyu Dong, Xi Zhang, and Chenxi Xiao†

† Corresponding author, School of Information Science and Technology, ShanghaiTech University

2026 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Biomimetics (IEEE ROBIO 2026)

PDF Video Code

Abstract

Vision-based tactile sensors capture contact appearance, geometry, indentation depth, and tangential deformation within a single image stream. Yet visuo-tactile policies often process these observations as ordinary images, making it difficult to determine which physical cues contribute to policy learning. We present a controlled real-robot study using an enhanced LeRobot-compatible visuo-tactile platform. From the same tactile stream, we derive tactile RGB images, normal/depth maps, and marker-flow fields, and evaluate them under identical demonstrations and the same policy backbone. Across 720 real-robot trials, tactile perception improves aggregate success from 57.8% to 78.9%. Marker flow is the strongest individual cue overall at 74.4% and plays an important role in force regulation after contact is established. Cue-factorized encoding consistently improves performance, while cross-attention offers a lightweight fusion alternative with performance comparable to tactile-pretrained encoding.

Controlled comparison of tactile representations

Conclusion

Tactile cues contribute at different interaction stages. Marker flow is particularly effective after contact is established, cue-factorized encoding improves performance, and cross-attention offers a lightweight fusion alternative with performance comparable to tactile-pretrained encoding.
